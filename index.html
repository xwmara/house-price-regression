What Drives House Prices in Ames, Iowa?

Log-linear regression, diagnostics and regularization on the Ames Housing data

This project explains and predicts the sale prices of 2,930 houses sold in Ames, Iowa, between 2006 and 2010. It estimates which characteristics of a house move its price, tests whether the regression assumptions hold, and measures how accurately the model prices houses it has not seen, in dollars.

Key results

  • The model prices new houses within about 11% on average. On a held-out test set of 881 houses, the mean absolute error is $18,700 and the model explains 87% of the price variation, against $37,722 and 50% for living area alone.
  • Quality, size and location drive prices. Each step on the 10-level quality scale adds about 11%, a 10% larger house costs about 4.4% more, and the same house costs about 37% more in Crawford than in Iowa DOT and Rail Road.
  • Room counts add little once size is known: their effects are small and not robust, and the simpler Lasso model drops them.
  • Ridge and Lasso do not beat OLS here: with 2,049 training houses and moderate multicollinearity, the OLS estimates are already stable.
  • The largest errors come from unusual sales. Only 24% of the houses with extreme residuals were normal sales, against 82% overall: foreclosures, family sales and unfinished houses sell far from their modeled value.

What the analysis covers

  • Data and EDA: variable selection, descriptive statistics, distributions and correlations, the log transform, a stratified 70/30 train/test split, and an exploratory segmentation with K-Means and PCA.
  • Models: simple and multiple OLS regression with percentage interpretation of log coefficients; RESET, Breusch-Pagan, Durbin-Watson and Jarque-Bera tests; robust and clustered standard errors; VIF; outliers and Cook’s distance; test-set evaluation in dollars with Duan’s smearing correction; scenario forecasts with normal and empirical prediction intervals; Ridge and Lasso with cross-validation.
  • Conclusions: findings, weaknesses of the model, uses in property valuation and credit risk, limitations and extensions.

All numbers in the text are computed in R when the report is rendered. The code is on GitHub.
